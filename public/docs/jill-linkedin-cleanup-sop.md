# SOP: Clean Up Joe's LinkedIn

**Owner:** Jill (executes) · **Reviewer:** Illy (approves deletions in batches) · **Account:** Joe Che, LinkedIn login in LastPass (`linkedin.com`, newyork1@gmail.com)

**Goal:** Joe's LinkedIn should read as one clear thing: the founder of Masterminds HQ who helps small business owners build AI operating systems. Anything that does not serve that story gets removed or hidden. When someone lands on his profile, every element should make him look like the person you would want to learn AI business systems from.

## The one rule for every decision

Ask: **does this help a small business owner trust Joe as their guide to AI and business systems?**
- Yes → keep it.
- No → flag it for removal.
- Not sure → leave it and add it to the "ask Joe" list. Never guess on deletions.

## What counts as business (KEEP)

- Anything about Masterminds HQ, the AI Business Automation Mastermind, or Level 1.
- AI, automation, agents, operating systems, productivity, systems for small business.
- Joe Che as founder, speaker, mentor, author (the AI OS book).
- All Sorted, the Connection Map, and his other current ventures.
- Posts that got real engagement and are on-topic (these are proof he is worth following).
- Genuine wins, client results, and testimonials.

## What to flag for removal (DELETE or HIDE)

- Off-topic personal content that does not fit the founder story (random reshares, memes, unrelated life updates).
- Old jobs, brands, or projects Joe has moved on from that confuse the current positioning.
- Anything half-finished, low quality, or that reads as spammy or salesy in a pushy way (Joe serves, he does not squeeze).
- Duplicate or near-empty posts.
- Endorsements or skills that are off-brand.

## The safe process (never delete without this)

You are NOT deleting anything directly at first. You are building a list, Illy approves it, THEN you delete.

1. **Do not delete anything yet.** Go section by section: Profile (headline, about, banner, featured), Experience, Education, Skills, then Activity/Posts.
2. For each item you think should go, add a row to the review sheet (below): what it is, a screenshot or link, and why it should be removed.
3. When you have a batch of about 15 to 20 items, send the list to **Illy** for approval.
4. Illy marks each row keep or delete.
5. You delete ONLY the approved rows. Hide (not delete) anything that is borderline but Illy is unsure about, so it is reversible.
6. Log what you deleted in the same sheet with the date, so there is a record.

## Review sheet format (one row per item)

| Item | Where (section) | Link/screenshot | Why remove | Illy: keep/delete | Done (date) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Hard guardrails

- **Never delete connections or contacts.** This is about content and positioning, not his network.
- **Never send messages, connection requests, or posts** from Joe's account during cleanup. You are tidying, not publishing.
- **Never delete anything not on an Illy-approved row.**
- If LinkedIn asks for a login code Joe needs to approve, message Illy so a live window with Joe can be arranged (see the social access package).
- When in doubt, hide instead of delete. Hidden is reversible, deleted often is not.

## What "done" looks like

Joe's profile top-to-bottom tells the AI-business-systems founder story with nothing off-message. Featured section shows his best on-brand proof (book, mastermind, top posts). The review sheet is a complete log of every change, approved by Illy.
