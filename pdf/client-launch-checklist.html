The Client
Launch Checklist

Turn a custom service into a packaged offer people can say yes to quickly. Launch a small paid program without waiting for a perfect funnel, and make the path from curious visitor to booked call clear and measurable.

Five steps. One page each.
Work through it in order and do not skip step one.

Step 1Name the one problem you solve

Most offers fail here, before any funnel is ever built. If a stranger cannot tell in one sentence what you fix, nothing downstream can rescue that.

  • Write the problem in the client's words, not yours. They do not want "strategic alignment". They want to stop losing a day a week to quoting.
  • Name who it is not for. An offer that serves everyone converts nobody. Excluding people is what makes the right person lean in.
  • State the before and after. Where they are on Monday, where they are after working with you. If you cannot describe the after concretely, the offer is not ready.
  • Say it out loud to one real person. If they ask a clarifying question, the sentence is not finished.
The trap You will be tempted to list everything you can do. Resist it. A packaged offer wins because it is narrow enough to be understood in five seconds, not because it is comprehensive.

Step 2Package it so a yes is easy

Custom work forces the buyer to design the deal themselves. That is work, and work is where deals die. Take the decision off their plate.

  • Fix the scope. Decide what is in and what is out, and write it down. Anything you leave vague you will end up doing for free.
  • Fix the price. One number, published. Custom quotes add a delay and a negotiation, and both cost you conversions.
  • Fix the timeline. "About six weeks" is not a timeline. "Three sessions across three weeks" is.
  • Give it a name. A named thing is a product. An unnamed thing is a conversation that has to start from zero every time.
  • Offer exactly one tier to begin with. Choice slows people down. Add tiers later, once you know what people actually buy.
Instead ofPackage it as
Consulting, hourly, scope to be discussedA named two week audit, one fixed price, one deliverable
Ongoing retainer, flexible hoursFour sessions a month, a fixed list of what is included
Custom build, quoted per projectA starter build at a set price, with extras priced separately

Step 3Build the shortest possible path to a yes

You do not need a funnel. You need a path. The difference is that a path can be finished this week.

  • One page, one action. The page explains the offer and asks for one thing. Every extra link is a way out.
  • Put the price on the page. Hiding it filters out serious buyers and attracts tyre kickers who wanted a free consult anyway.
  • Make the call button the only button. If a visitor has to hunt for how to buy, they will not hunt.
  • Answer the three objections in writing. Too expensive, no time, tried something like this before. Answer them on the page so you do not have to answer them on the call.
  • Ship it before it is finished. A live page you improve weekly beats a perfect page that launches next quarter.
Rule of thumb If the path from landing on your page to booking a call takes more than two clicks, it is too long. Count the clicks. Most people are surprised.

Step 4Make it measurable

If you cannot see where people stop, you will improve the wrong thing. Two numbers are enough to start. Do not build a dashboard.

  • Count visitors and count booked calls. That ratio is your whole business, expressed in one number.
  • Watch the drop, not the total. A hundred visitors and no calls is a page problem. Ten calls and no sales is an offer problem. They need opposite fixes.
  • Write down the number every Friday. A number you check once a quarter is decoration. A number you check weekly changes behaviour.
  • Change one thing at a time. If you rewrite the headline and the price in the same week, you learn nothing from either.

Calls, no salesThe offer is clear but not compellingScope, price, or who it is for

Step 5Launch small, on purpose

Waiting for the perfect launch is how offers die quietly. A small paid launch teaches you more in a week than planning does in a month.

  • Sell it to five people before you build the whole thing. Five paying clients will tell you what to build. An empty roadmap will not.
  • Charge from the first one. Free pilots teach you what people will accept, not what they will pay for. Those are different lessons.
  • Tell people it is the first cohort. Being early is a feature. It buys you room to be imperfect, and honest buyers respect it.
  • Deliver it manually the first time. Automate only what hurts after you have felt it hurt.
  • Book the next launch date before this one ends. Momentum is easier to keep than to restart.

If you only do one thing

Do step one, properly, this week. Write the single sentence that names the problem you solve and who it is for, and say it out loud to a real person. Everything else in this checklist gets easier once that sentence is true, and nothing else works until it is.
